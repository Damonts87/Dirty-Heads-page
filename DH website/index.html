Dirty Heads formed in Huntington Beach, California, when childhood friends Jared “Dirty J” Watson and Dustin “Duddy B” Bushnell began blending hip-hop vocals with reggae, punk, and rock influences. Their DIY hustle—burning CDs, playing small local shows, and leaning into the Southern California surf-and-skate scene—helped them build a grassroots following long before major-label attention. That early mix of laid-back reggae grooves and energetic rap verses became their signature, setting them apart from both traditional reggae bands and straight hip-hop acts.

After breaking through with Any Port in a Storm (2008) and the fan-favorite Cabin by the Sea (2012), Dirty Heads steadily evolved their sound while keeping the feel-good core intact. Albums like Sound of Change and Dirty Heads pushed them toward a more radio-friendly alternative rock polish, while Swim Team and Super Moon leaned back into beachy reggae and pop hooks. Across each release, they’ve balanced catchy choruses with genre-hopping production, which is why tracks like “Vacation,” “Lay Me Down,” and “Sloth’s Revenge” can all feel different yet unmistakably Dirty Heads.

Both frontmen stay busy outside the main band: Duddy B releases music under his solo project Duddy B, exploring more personal and acoustic-leaning material, while Dirty J has collaborated with producers and artists across reggae and hip-hop. As a band, Dirty Heads are frequent collaborators in the modern reggae-rock scene, teaming up with artists like Rome Ramirez, Pepper, Slightly Stoopid, and Sublime With Rome. These partnerships keep their sound fresh and connected to a wider community, reinforcing Dirty Heads’ role as a bridge between reggae, rock, and hip-hop cultures.
